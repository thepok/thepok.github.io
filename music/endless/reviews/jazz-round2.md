# Independent musical review — round 2

Evidence: twelve consecutive revised outputs at the same session seed100, compiled track events and tune/performance records, plus 48 independent opening plans (seeds0..47). Reports: `round2-audit.json`, `openings-round2.json`. This is score/event analysis, not human listening.

## Verdict

**Materially improved against round1.** The changes directly address the listener's concern that an endless stream should not be judged only by individual pieces. I support moving this candidate to browser/audio validation and publication once balance/render checks pass. I would call it a substantially more varied jazz quartet generator, not proven mastery.

## Compared with round1

- The two sets no longer repeat their six-slot feel/layout/form sequence. Twelve outputs still cover all five feels, three forms and eight tonic/mode combinations.
- Across48 independently seeded openings: 21 straight swing, 10 shuffle, 7 bossa, 5 ballad, 5 waltz. All four solo layouts appear (18 piano-sax,14 sax-piano,9 trading,7 bass-dialogue); intro piano/bass37/11, initial head piano/sax23/25. Thus repeated starts/skips no longer force one opening.
- Solo extent varies: one or two sections per voice. The two ballads shortened from329.55/294.4 seconds to257.39/217.6 seconds. They give a clearer release in the programme.
- Returns vary between whole head, last half and last part. Exact raw reprise/head matches fell from24/24 sections to11/19; the final reprise phrase is now paraphrased in a recognizably related way.
- Intro instrumentation now varies and drums wait for the head. Ending is one intentional final sonority, with held/stop/tag differences instead of ongoing generic comping.
- Solo material has a per-song developmental direction (register growth, temporal expansion, displaced answer) instead of identical motif policy throughout every song.
- Sax remains cleanly monophonic in the checked events (zero >0.04-beat overlaps). Large within-section leaps decreased from10 to4 across twelve pieces. The broader contour-safe fit prevents the explicit per-note octave folding fallback of round1.

## What is still missing for mastery?

1. Solo direction is still a small vocabulary of algorithmic transforms. A human soloist can prepare an idea over several phrases, choose a surprising interruption and answer a drummer's specific gesture. These outputs provide basic development, not that level of spontaneous dialogue.
2. Bass solos and traded fours are currently preassigned set-layout features; the drummer supports sections but does not explicitly exchange solo fours with the horns. There is room for stronger conversational authorship without increasing activity everywhere.
3. Every complete set includes the same count of stylistic families. Order and local shape now vary, but much longer listening may still recognize the programme's palette.
4. The rare compressed-register fallback preserves contour and a *global* pitch-class vocabulary, not necessarily each note's harmonic function under its current chord. Generated reviewed lines show no conspicuous leap defect, but narrow-register composition would be a stronger future design than posthoc fitting.
5. The review cannot settle lead audibility, sax tone hardness, brush balance or emotional effect without rendered-audio evidence and listener feedback. Publication wording should not promise mastery.

No further score-level blocker identified in this12-piece comparison. Keep release gating on non-silent stable playback, valid samples, sensible lead-to-accompaniment balance, recipe reproduction and the skip/seek regressions.
